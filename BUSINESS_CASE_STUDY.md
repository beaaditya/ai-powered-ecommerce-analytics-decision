## Executive Summary

Modern retail enterprises manage vast volumes of transaction logs, customer demographics, promotion schedules, and marketing campaign histories. However, traditional Business Intelligence (BI) tools and static dashboarding architectures create a significant friction layer between business decision-makers and raw data. Category managers, merchandising directors, and marketing executives are frequently constrained by pre-aggregated charts, rigid filters, and multi-day turnaround times for ad-hoc SQL requests.

This business case study presents the design, technical implementation, and empirical business analytics of the **AI-Assisted Retail Intelligence System**. Built upon the actual longitudinal data of the **Dunnhumby "The Complete Journey"** dataset (tracking 2,500 shopper households, 92,339 stock-keeping units (SKUs), and over $8.05M in verified sales across 102 weeks), the platform combines robust relational data engineering in PostgreSQL with a deterministic multi-agent AI copilot powered by Google Gemini.

The system delivers real-time KPI monitoring across five dedicated dashboard environments, automated background detection of revenue anomalies and customer churn exposure, natural-language-to-SQL conversational query answering with strict safety gating, and automated executive report synthesis—reconciled directly against relational ground truth with zero data hallucination.

## 1. Business Problem

### 1.1 The Retail Decision-Making Challenge
A large-scale grocery and consumer goods retailer operates in an environment characterized by high transaction frequency, low individual product margins, intense promotional volatility, and evolving customer shopping habits. To maintain profitability and market share, retail leadership must continuously answer complex, interrelated questions across seven core business dimensions:

```
                          ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                          │     Core Retail Business Dimensions     │
                          └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                               │
     ┌───────────────────┬─────────────────────┼─────────────────────┬───────────────────┐
     ▼                   ▼                     ▼                     ▼                   ▼
┌──────────────┐  ┌──────────────┐      ┌──────────────┐      ┌──────────────┐    ┌──────────────┐
│   Customer   │  │   Customer   │      │   Product    │      │  Marketing   │    │  Promotion   │
│  Behaviour   │  │    Value     │      │ Performance  │      │  Campaigns   │    │Effectiveness │
└──────────────┘  └──────────────┘      └──────────────┘      └──────────────┘    └──────────────┘
```

1. **Customer Behaviour:** How frequently do distinct shopper cohorts visit the physical store? How do basket sizes, product diversity, and trip cadence evolve across the customer lifecycle?
2. **Customer Value:** What is the monetary lifetime value of different demographic groups? How concentrated is total store revenue among top-tier spenders?
3. **Product Performance:** Which departments, commodity categories, and individual SKUs drive the bulk of sales volume versus revenue? Which categories are gaining half-over-half momentum versus contracting?
4. **Sales Trends:** What are the macro revenue trajectories over 102 calendar weeks? How do seasonal velocity changes impact weekly unit throughput and average basket value?
5. **Campaign Performance:** Which multi-channel marketing campaigns (direct mailings, targeted coupons) generate the highest household reach, redemption rates, and engagement?
6. **Coupon Redemption:** How do coupon redemptions distribute across household income levels, family sizes, and purchase categories?
7. **Promotion Effectiveness:** What incremental sales lift is generated by in-store displays, front-page mailers, and temporary price reductions versus baseline sales?

### 1.2 Limitations of Traditional Static BI Reporting
While traditional reporting systems (such as periodic PDF slide decks, pre-built static dashboards, or static spreadsheet dumps) provide retrospective visibility, they exhibit fundamental deficiencies for modern agile decision-making:

* **Inflexible Drill-Down:** Static charts only answer the specific questions for which they were hardcoded. If a category manager notices a sudden revenue drop in Week 100, a static chart cannot explain *why* without a separate engineering ticket.
* **Interpretation Latency:** Business users must manually hunt through dozens of tabs, memorize metric definitions, and mentally cross-reference customer cohorts with promotional calendars.
* **SQL Bottlenecks:** Non-technical domain experts must rely on centralized data engineering teams to construct ad-hoc queries, introducing turnaround delays of days or weeks.
* **Passive Rather Than Proactive:** Traditional BI sits passively until opened. It does not automatically notify managers when a 585-household high-value customer cohort enters an at-risk churn state or when a category contracts by over 20%.

### 3.2 Detailed Entity Descriptions

1. **Transactions (`raw.transaction_data` / `clean.transaction_data`):**
   Contains 2,595,734 line-item purchase records. Each record details the `household_key`, unique `basket_id`, transaction `day` (1–719), `week_no` (1–102), `product_id`, `quantity`, actual `sales_value` ($), `store_id`, `retail_disc` (store loyalty discount), `coupon_disc` (manufacturer coupon deduction), and `coupon_match_disc` (retailer matched coupon deduction).
2. **Households & Demographics (`raw.hh_demographic` / `clean.hh_demographic`):**
   Covers demographic profiles for 801 demographic-surveyed households (out of 2,500 total active purchasing households), capturing household size, age bracket, estimated income group, homeowner status, marital status, and presence of children.
3. **Products & Hierarchy (`raw.product` / `clean.product`):**
   Contains 92,339 distinct SKUs mapped across 29 major store departments (`department`), 300+ merchandise categories (`commodity_desc`), 2,200+ sub-commodities (`sub_commodity_desc`), and brand classification (`brand`: National vs. Private Label).
4. **Marketing Campaigns (`raw.campaign_desc` & `raw.campaign_table`):**
   Details 30 distinct marketing initiatives spanning 7,208 direct household drop assignments across three strategic campaign tiers:
   * *TypeA:* High-intensity, multi-coupon promotional mailings.
   * *TypeB:* Medium-intensity category-focused coupon drops.
   * *TypeC:* Seasonal or specialized targeted coupon distributions.
5. **Coupons & Redemptions (`raw.coupon` & `raw.coupon_redempt`):**
   Maps 124,550 coupon-to-product relationships across campaigns and tracks 2,318 distinct household coupon redemption events with exact day timestamps.
6. **Promotions & Causal Data (`raw.causal_data` / `clean.causal_data`):**
   Comprises 36,786,526 weekly store-product causal entries recording whether an item was featured in physical store displays (front of store, end-cap, lobby) or promotional print mailers (Page A, Page B, etc.).

### 5.1 Detailed Methodological Breakdown

#### 1. Revenue & Sales Velocity
* **Business Question:** What is the total dollar revenue generated, and how does sales velocity fluctuate across the 102-week operational window?
* **Methodology:** Time-series aggregation of `sales_value` in `analytics.weekly_metrics`, calculating rolling 4-week moving averages, weekly minimums, and peak velocities.
* **Business Purpose:** Identify macro growth trajectories, seasonal demand spikes, and overall business health.

#### 2. Customer Value & Penetration
* **Business Question:** How much revenue does each customer household contribute, and what is the distribution of spending across the customer base?
* **Methodology:** Aggregation of household monetary totals into discrete value tiers (Under $500, $500–$1.5k, $1.5k–$3k, $3k–$5k, $5k–$10k, Over $10k).
* **Business Purpose:** Quantify customer acquisition and lifetime value distribution.

#### 3. Basket Metrics & Transaction Density
* **Business Question:** What is the typical transaction size, and how does item depth correlate with spend per basket?
* **Methodology:** Calculation of Average Basket Value ($/basket = total revenue / total distinct baskets) and units per transaction across 276,484 completed shopping trips.
* **Business Purpose:** Guide store layout, checkout merchandising, and minimum-spend promotional thresholds.

#### 4. Purchase Frequency & Store Loyalty
* **Business Question:** How frequently do households return to the store, and what proportion of customers are repeat shoppers?
* **Methodology:** Evaluating distinct shopping visit days per household and calculating the repeat customer rate (percentage of households with $\ge 10$ store visits).
* **Business Purpose:** Distinguish habitual regular shoppers from transient one-time visitors.

#### 5. Department, Category & SKU Pareto Performance
* **Business Question:** Which departments and categories generate the majority of sales, and how concentrated is revenue among top individual SKUs?
* **Methodology:** Department and commodity revenue ranking alongside Pareto cumulative share calculation for the top 10, 50, and 100 SKUs out of 92,339 catalog items.
* **Business Purpose:** Prioritize inventory management, vendor negotiations, and shelf-space allocation.

#### 6. RFM Customer Segmentation
* **Business Question:** How can 2,500 distinct shopper households be classified into actionable behavioral cohorts based on recency, frequency, and monetary spend?
* **Methodology:** Scoring households on a 1–5 scale for Recency (days since last purchase), Frequency (distinct visit days), and Monetary Value (total spend) using quintile bucketing, assigning them into six core segments: *Champions*, *Loyal Customers*, *Recent Customers*, *Regular Customers*, *At Risk High Value*, and *At Risk*.
* **Business Purpose:** Enable differentiated marketing strategies, loyalty incentives, and targeted retention programs.

#### 7. Customer Journey & Spending Momentum
* **Business Question:** Which customer cohorts are accelerating their store spend versus declining in engagement over time?
* **Methodology:** Half-over-half spend comparison dividing the 102-week timeline into H1 (Weeks 1–51) and H2 (Weeks 52–102) to classify households into *Growing*, *Stable*, and *Declining* trajectories.
* **Business Purpose:** Trigger proactive intervention before declining high-value shoppers fully lapse.

#### 8. Marketing Campaign Efficacy
* **Business Question:** How effective are structured marketing campaigns at driving household participation and coupon redemptions?
* **Methodology:** Campaign-level conversion analysis in `analytics.campaign_performance` measuring targeted households, redeeming households, and the overall redemption rate percentage ($(\text{redeemed} / \text{targeted}) \times 100$).
* **Business Purpose:** Determine ROI across campaign formats (TypeA vs. TypeB vs. TypeC) to optimize direct-mail marketing budgets.

#### 9. Coupon Redemption & Household Response
* **Business Question:** Do households that redeem coupons generate higher overall basket spend and incremental revenue lift during active campaigns?
* **Methodology:** Pre- vs. post-campaign spend differential analysis in `analytics.campaign_customer_spend` and `analytics.customer_campaign_response`.
* **Business Purpose:** Validate whether coupon discounts drive incremental basket expansion or merely subsidize purchases that would have occurred anyway.

#### 10. Promotion Effectiveness (In-Store Display & Mailers)
* **Business Question:** What is the relative sales and volume contribution of products featured on physical end-cap displays versus print mailers?
* **Methodology:** Aggregation of promoted versus non-promoted item performance in `analytics.promotion_sales`, segmenting volume by display type and mailer placement.
* **Business Purpose:** Optimize promotional allowances, circular ad pricing, and end-cap space allocation.

The artificial intelligence layer transforms the relational database into an interactive, natural-language conversational decision-support copilot.

```
                             ▼

```

### 6.1 Core AI Components

#### 1. Natural Language to SQL Generation (`backend/sql_agent.py`)
Provides prompt-engineered schema context for 13 analytics tables to Google Gemini. Gemini generates syntactically valid PostgreSQL queries without hallucinating table or column names.

#### 2. SQL Safety Validation Gate (`backend/sql_agent.py`)
Every query generated by the AI passes through a deterministic Python regex safety validator before database execution:
* Requires queries to start with `SELECT` or `WITH`.
* Blocks destructive commands: `DROP`, `DELETE`, `UPDATE`, `INSERT`, `ALTER`, `TRUNCATE`, `CREATE`, `GRANT`, `REVOKE`, `MERGE`, `COPY`, `CALL`, `EXECUTE`, `EXEC`.
* Prohibits stacked multi-statement execution by rejecting internal semicolons.
* Executes within read-only PostgreSQL transaction sessions (`readonly=True`, `autocommit=True`).

#### 3. Automated Business Insights Engine (`backend/insights.py`)
Runs background anomaly and trend detection across six business domains without requiring user prompting:
1. *Revenue Trend:* Scans rolling 4-week revenue moving averages.
2. *Customer Trend:* Identifies shifts in active household shopping participation.
3. *Category Momentum:* Flags top growing ($\ge +15\%$) and contracting ($\le -15\%$) categories.
4. *Campaign Efficacy:* Highlights campaigns exceeding high-conversion benchmarks ($\ge 10\%$).
5. *Promotional Impact:* Computes revenue and volume generated through promoted items.
6. *Segment Exposure:* Detects revenue concentration in high-value at-risk cohorts.

#### 4. AI Business Analyst Agent (`backend/agent.py`)
An autonomous multi-step diagnostic agent that handles complex, open-ended business inquiries (e.g., *"Why did sales decline?"*). The agent formulates a multi-step investigation plan, queries revenue, customer, product, and marketing tools sequentially, evaluates collected evidence, and delivers a diagnostic narrative with prioritized recommendations.

#### 5. Automated Analysis Pipeline (`backend/pipeline.py`)
An autonomous scheduled diagnostic pipeline that evaluates all four retail domains simultaneously, compiles 12+ structured findings, identifies high-priority business risks and growth opportunities, and provides an in-memory cached executive briefing.

#### 6. AI Business Reports (`backend/reports.py`)
An automated executive report generator that aggregates metrics across all database modules, injects live context into an executive reporting prompt, and produces structured markdown reports with executive summaries, domain breakdowns, and strategic action plans.

### 7.1 Revenue & Velocity Findings
* **Total Store Revenue:** Over the 102-week evaluation period, total cumulative revenue reached **$8,057,463.08** across **260,685,622** units sold.
* **Average Basket Value:** Across 276,484 completed shopping transactions, the store achieved an average basket spend of **$29.14**.
* **Recent Velocity Trend:** In the most recent 4-week window (Weeks 99–102), average weekly revenue was **$87,700.73**, representing a **-3.49%** contraction compared to the prior 4-week average of **$90,873.51** (Weeks 95–98).

### 7.2 Customer & Demographic Findings
* **Customer Base & Loyalty:** The store tracks **2,500** unique households with an average spend of **$3,222.99** per household and a high **96.1%** repeat customer rate ($\ge 10$ shopping trips).
* **Active Customer Participation:** Weekly active shopping households averaged **1,278** in recent weeks, reflecting a **-4.63%** contraction compared to **1,340** active households in the preceding period.
* **Lifetime Frequency:** Shopper households averaged **108.6** distinct transaction trips over the 102-week timeframe.

### 7.3 Product & Assortment Findings
* **Department Revenue Dominance:** The **Grocery** department is the primary revenue engine, generating **$4,093,814.14** (**50.81%** of total store sales) across **29,819,657** units.
* **Secondary Departments:** Drug GM generated **$1,152,060.03**, Produce generated **$664,599.98**, and Meat / Packaged Meat combined for **$1,290,089.36**.
* **High-Volume Traffic Driver:** **Kiosk-Gas** accounted for **216,488,435** units (**83.05%** of all units sold across the retailer) while contributing **$544,222.28** in revenue, highlighting fuel as the primary physical foot-traffic anchor.
* **Top Commodity Categories:** **Soft Drinks** led all merchandise categories with **$327,647.30** in sales, followed closely by **Beef** (**$312,480.90**).
* **Category Growth Divergence:** Between H1 and H2, the **Garden Center** category surged **+144.77%** (from $2,078.10 in H1 to $5,086.56 in H2, a +$3,008.46 gain), whereas **Corn in Produce** contracted **-21.33%** (from $3,042.07 in H1 to $2,393.31 in H2, a -$648.76 drop).
* **Pareto Concentration:** The top 100 SKUs (out of 92,339 catalog items) generated **$1,476,346.76** (**18.32%** of total store sales), while the top 10 SKUs accounted for **$292,854.12** (**3.63%** of total sales).

### 7.4 Marketing & Campaign Findings
* **Overall Campaign Volume:** Across 30 targeted marketing campaigns, **7,208** household drop mailings yielded **2,318** coupon redemptions, representing an average redemption rate of **9.11%**.
* **Top Performing Campaign:** **Campaign 18** (TypeA, Days 587–642) achieved the highest individual engagement with an **18.89%** household redemption rate (**214** redeeming households out of **1,133** targeted, totaling **653** redemptions).
* **Campaign Tier Effectiveness:** **TypeA** campaigns (intensive direct mailings) demonstrated the highest average conversion rate at **14.22%**, significantly outperforming **TypeB** (**9.7%**) and **TypeC** (**6.1%**).
* **Promoted Campaign Spend Lift:** Targeted marketing campaigns generated **$1,524,449.60** in observed promotional spend lift across participating shopper households.

### 7.5 Promotion & Causal Findings
* **Promotional Sales Volume:** Items supported by in-store displays and circular mailers generated **$1,559,228.97** in revenue across **796,129** units sold at an average item price of **$1.96**.
* **Display vs. Mailer Impact:** Combining physical front-of-store end-cap displays with circular feature mailers resulted in higher unit velocity compared to standalone circular advertising.

### 7.6 Customer RFM Segments & Churn Risk
* **RFM Distribution:** The 2,500 households are segmented into six behavioral groups:
  * *Champions:* **119** households averaging **$5,137.30** in lifetime spend.
  * *Loyal Customers:* **623** households with consistent high-frequency spend.
  * *Recent Customers:* **654** households representing newer onboarding opportunities.
  * *Regular Customers:* **119** households with moderate trip cadence.
  * *At Risk High Value:* **585** households (23.4% of customer base) accounting for **$3,990,569.06** in historical spend (averaging **$6,821.49** per household).
  * *At Risk (Regular):* **415** households accounting for **$647,811.97** in historical spend.
* **Total Revenue at Risk:** Combined, the 1,000 at-risk households (**40.0%** of customer base) represent **$4,638,381.03** (**57.57%** of total store historical revenue), highlighting customer retention as the single largest financial exposure facing the business.

## 12. Technical & Methodological Limitations

To maintain objective engineering rigor, several technical and methodological limitations must be documented:

1. **Historical Observational Dataset:** The Dunnhumby dataset represents historical supermarket transactions from a specific two-year period. Consumer behaviors, inflation dynamics, and digital purchasing patterns have evolved since data collection.
2. **Correlation vs. Causation:** Observational analytics (such as measuring spend lift after campaign drops) demonstrate statistical correlation but do not prove strict counterfactual causation without controlled A/B experimentation.
3. **Demographic Coverage:** Complete demographic profiles are available for 801 of the 2,500 households, meaning demographic-specific drill-downs represent a surveyed subset rather than the entire population.
4. **AI Generation Oversight:** While natural-language-to-SQL translation is governed by strict AST safety validators and grounded prompts, natural language outputs require periodic human-in-the-loop review for edge-case interpretations.
5. **Scale & Production Deployment:** The current implementation is optimized for an on-premise or single-node deployment. Multi-tenant enterprise deployment would require horizontal connection pooling (e.g., PgBouncer), role-based access control (RBAC), and distributed Redis caching.
